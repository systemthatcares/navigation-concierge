## Context

This is the homework for Block 1 of the Navigation Concierge build. The asked-for artifact is a sketch combining:

- **Four options:** COBRA, ACA marketplace SEP, spouse plan via SEP, short-term health insurance (plus "going bare" as a fifth de facto option)
- **Decision compressors:** household income projection, in-progress care, prescription costs, spouse with employer benefits, time until new employer coverage

The conceptual unlock for me: the tree and the compressors are not on the same axis. The tree is the map (which paths are even alive given the timing window). The compressors are the compass (how to rank the live paths against the client's specific situation). They are two phases, not one nested structure.

This memo is also the source material for Shota's profiling agent — the compressors are the fields the agent needs to capture from a client to drive a recommendation.

---

## Phase 1: The Tree (timing gates)

```
Day 0: Coverage loss event
│
├── Day 0–30
│   All four paths potentially live:
│   COBRA, ACA SEP, Spouse SEP, Short-term*
│
├── Day 31–60
│   COBRA, ACA SEP, Short-term* live
│   (Spouse SEP usually closed at 30, plan-specific)
│
└── Day 61+
    Short-term* or wait for ACA Open Enrollment or bare
    (COBRA election + ACA SEP both closed)

*Short-term not available in CA (SB 910, 2018).
 For CA clients: collapse to COBRA / ACA / Spouse / bare.
```

### Window specifics

| Path | Window | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| COBRA | 60 days from coverage loss (or notice of right, whichever later) | Election can be retroactive |
| ACA Marketplace SEP | 60 days from loss; also up to 60 days *before* anticipated loss | |
| Spouse plan SEP | Typically 30 days, plan-specific (some give 60) | Tightest window |
| Short-term | Year-round in states that allow it | **Banned in CA** |
| Bare | No window | No federal mandate penalty since 2019; **CA has a state mandate** |

## Phase 2: The Compressor Scorecard

For each path still alive after Phase 1, the compressors push it up or down. Read a row as: "if this is true for the client, here's how it weights each option."

| Compressor | COBRA | ACA SEP | Spouse SEP | Short-term | Bare |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spouse benefits + SEP open | — | — | **++** default winner | — | — |
| In-progress care (active Tx, surgery, pregnancy) | **++** network continuity | – network risk | – network risk | – – excludes pre-existing | – – catastrophic |
| Specialty Rx on current formulary | **++** preserved | verify | verify | – – minimal Rx | – – |
| Household income, ACA subsidy-eligible | – full premium | **++** subsidized | – subsidy lost | n/a | n/a |
| New coverage <90 days away | **+** month-to-month bridge | – overkill | – overkill | **++** bridge purpose | – risky |

---

## Heuristics that Fall Out of the Scorecard

1. **Spouse plan available + SEP open** → default to spouse. Override only if cost or network is a dealbreaker.
2. **In-progress care** → COBRA almost always wins. Network continuity is worth the premium for someone mid-treatment.
3. **No in-progress care, no spouse plan, income low** → ACA subsidy can make a silver plan dramatically cheaper than COBRA.
4. **New job coverage in <90 days, no active care** → short-term bridge (non-CA), or month-to-month COBRA (you can elect COBRA and drop it when new coverage starts).
5. **Day 60+ with none of the above** → narrow set: short-term (non-CA), wait for Open Enrollment, or bare with CA mandate penalty.

## Question Sequence (Information per Turn)

The scorecard tells the agent how each input weights the options. It does not tell the agent which question to ask first. Order matters because some questions are gates (they eliminate whole branches before the next question is even relevant) and some are short-circuits (they can end the consult early with a high-confidence recommendation).

The sequence below is ordered by **information gained per question**, highest first.

### Stage 1: Gate questions (eliminate dead branches before scoring)

1. **Days since coverage loss?**
   Eliminates branches by timing window before any compressor matters. A Day-75 client does not need to discuss spouse SEPs.

2. **State of residence?**
   Collapses short-term in or out. CA clients lose short-term entirely, which simplifies the rest of the consult.

3. **Household composition?**
   Sets the frame for spouse-plan and dependent-coverage questions. If single, the spouse SEP branch is dead.

### Stage 2: Short-circuit questions (may end the consult early)

4. **Spouse with employer benefits, and is the SEP window still open?**
   If yes, spouse plan is the default recommendation in most situations. The remaining questions are confirmation, not exploration.

5. **In-progress care: active treatment, scheduled surgery, pregnancy, oncology follow-up, chronic specialist management?**
   If yes, COBRA is the default recommendation. Network continuity outweighs cost for someone mid-treatment. Remaining questions confirm whether anything overrides that.

If either short-circuit fires with high confidence, the agent can move to recommendation and skip Stage 3. If neither fires, continue.

### Stage 3: Ranking questions (rank the survivors)

6. **Projected household income, next 12 months?**
   Determines ACA subsidy magnitude. Can flip ACA from "more expensive than COBRA" to "much cheaper than COBRA."

7. **Specialty prescriptions and current formulary tier?**
   Refines COBRA versus ACA versus spouse on continuity of access, not just continuity of network.

8. **New employer coverage expected, and when?**
   Opens the bridge-coverage path (short-term in non-CA, or month-to-month COBRA). Only relevant for clients with a known landing date.

9. **Current employer's total premium?**
   Sizes the actual dollar cost of COBRA (102% of total). Many clients underestimate by 3-5x because they only know the employee-share number.

### Stage 4: Output

10. Run the scorecard with the populated profile. Produce window status, scoring, and recommended path plus next actions.

### Why this order

A naive agent asks all the compressor questions in scorecard order, then runs the math. That works, but it wastes the client's time on questions that could not have changed the answer. The ordering above front-loads the questions with the highest branch-elimination power, so a high-confidence consult can finish in 5 questions instead of 10, and a low-confidence consult still gets all 10 but in an order that makes the reasoning legible to the client as it unfolds.

## Consult Deliverable Structure

A consult should produce three things for the client:

1. **Window status:** which paths are still open given days since coverage loss
2. **Compressor scoring:** the scorecard filled in for the client's specific situation
3. **Recommended path + 2–3 concrete actions this week** (e.g., "elect COBRA at portal X by date Y," or "enroll on Covered California by Z")

## For Shota — Profile Fields the Agent Needs to Capture

These are the fields a profiling agent would need to populate before the scorecard can be run. Grouping by acquisition difficulty so Shota can prioritize the interview flow.

### Tier 1: easy / required upfront
- Date of coverage loss (or anticipated loss date)
- State of residence
- Household composition (self / spouse / dependents)

### Tier 2: structured ask, client knows the answer
- Spouse employed with benefits? Y/N
- Spouse plan SEP window (days from qualifying event)
- New job / new coverage expected? If yes, expected start date
- Currently uninsured between jobs?

### Tier 3: requires client effort or follow-up
- In-progress care: active treatment, scheduled surgery, pregnancy, oncology follow-up, chronic condition management
- Current providers (PCP, specialists) and which networks they accept
- Specialty Rx list with current formulary tier
- Projected household income next 12 months (for ACA subsidy modeling)
- Current employer's total premium (for COBRA cost projection — 102% of total)

### Tier 4: derived, not asked
- ACA subsidy eligibility band (derived from income + household size + state)
- CA mandate penalty exposure (derived from state + income)
- Whether short-term is even on the menu (derived from state)

## Open Verification Items (before client-facing)

- **2026 ACA subsidy structure.** IRA-enhanced subsidies were extended through 2025. Whether they continue into 2026 changes the math on path 3 dramatically.
- **CA mandate penalty 2026 amount.** Changes annually.
- **Spouse SEP window per plan.** 30 days is typical but plan-specific. Always have the client pull their spouse's SPD or call the plan.
- **COBRA premium math.** 102% of total premium (employee + employer share). Many clients don't realize how much their employer was subsidizing.
- **Covered California specifics.** Enrollment portal, document requirements, processing time. This is the CA-resident default and worth being fluent on.

## Why the Tree + Scorecard Are Separated

Originally I tried to nest the compressors inside the tree as sub-branches. It collapsed because the compressors interact: a spouse plan beats COBRA on cost but loses to COBRA on in-progress care. The same compressor produces opposite recommendations depending on which other compressors are also true.

Splitting it into two phases — tree eliminates dead branches by timing, scorecard ranks the live ones — is what made it tractable. That structure also matches the natural shape of the consult: "here's what's available to you, and here's how your situation ranks them."
